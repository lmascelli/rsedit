NAME
    editing-index - every function that moves, reads or changes the text

DESCRIPTION
    Every function that moves point, reads the text of a buffer or changes
    it, grouped by what it is for, with a few words on each. This page is
    a map, not the reference: each group says which page explains its
    functions, with their examples and their limits, and C-h f on a name
    gives its exact arguments.

    Commands and plain functions are listed together. A command can also
    be run with M-x or bound to a key; the keys that run one are on its
    page, and C-h w names them. The variables that change how these
    functions behave are on the same pages, and are not listed here.

    Where a function takes a count, N, it does its work N times, 1 by
    default, and a negative N goes the other way.

MOVING POINT
    On the `editing' page:

        forward-char              a character forward
        backward-char             a character back
        next-line                 a line down, keeping the column
        previous-line             a line up, keeping the column
        forward-word              past the end of the next word
        backward-word             to the start of the word before
        forward-paragraph         to the blank line after the paragraph
        backward-paragraph        to the blank line before it
        forward-sexp              over the next balanced expression
        backward-sexp             back over the one before
        up-list                   out of the list, past its closer
        backward-up-list          back to the start of the list
        down-list                 just inside the next list
        beginning-of-line         to the start of the line
        end-of-line               to the end of the line
        beginning-of-buffer       to the start of the buffer
        end-of-buffer             to the end of the buffer
        goto-char                 to POSITION
        goto-line                 to the start of line N

    On the `indentation' page:

        back-to-indentation       to the first character that is not blank

WHERE POINT IS
    On the `editing' page:

        point                     where point is, counted from 0
        point-min                 the first position, 0
        point-max                 the position after the last character
        line-number-at-point      the number of point's line, from 1
        current-line              the text of point's line

    On the `indentation' page:

        current-column            the column point is on, from 0

READING THE TEXT

        buffer-string             the whole text of the buffer
        buffer-substring          the text between START and END

    On the `completion' page:

        bounds-of-thing-at-point  where the symbol at point begins and ends
        buffer-words              the distinct words of a buffer

    On the `regexps' page:

        scan-buffer               every match of a pattern, and where

    On the `writing-modes' page:

        syntax-ppss               whether point is in a list, string or
                                  comment
        bounds-of-enclosing-list  where the list around point begins and
                                  ends
        balance-point             where every list open at a position
                                  has closed

    On the `editing' page:

        insert                    strings, one after another
        insert-newline            a newline, not indented
        open-line-above           blank lines above this one, indented

    On the `indentation' page:

DELETING
    Nothing deleted goes on the kill ring.
    On the `editing' page:

        delete-char               the N characters after point
        delete-backward-char      the character before point
        delete-region             the text between FROM and TO
        delete-blank-lines        the blank lines around here, but one
        just-one-space            the spaces around point, but one
        delete-horizontal-space   the spaces and tabs around point

    On the `buffers' page:

        clear-buffer              the whole text of the buffer

KILLING AND YANKING
    What a kill takes goes on the kill ring, for C-y to put back.
    On the `killing' page:

        kill-line                 to the end of the line
        kill-whole-line           the whole line, with its newline
        kill-word                 to the end of the Nth word
        backward-kill-word        back to the start of the Nth word
        kill-sexp                 over N balanced expressions
        backward-kill-sexp        back over N balanced expressions
        kill-paragraph            to the end of the paragraph
        backward-kill-paragraph   back to the start of the paragraph
        kill-region               the region
        kill-ring-save            the region, without deleting it
        kill-new                  a string, as if it had been killed
        yank                      insert the latest kill
        yank-pop                  swap what was yanked for the kill before
        current-kill              the kill N back, changing nothing
        kill-ring-length          how many kills the ring holds
        set-kill-ring-max         how many kills it may hold
        clipboard-sync-toggle     keep kills out of the system clipboard,
                                  or let them in again

RECTANGLES
    A rectangle is the block of columns between point and the mark.
    On the `rectangles' page:

        rectangle-mark-mode       show the region as a block, or stop
        kill-rectangle            delete the block, and keep it
        copy-rectangle-as-kill    keep the block, without deleting it
        delete-rectangle          delete the block, keeping nothing
        yank-rectangle            insert the block kept last
        open-rectangle            push the block right with spaces
        clear-rectangle           blank the block out with spaces
        string-rectangle          put STRING on every line of the block
        rectangle-bounds          the lines and columns of the block
        killed-rectangle          the block kept last, as strings
