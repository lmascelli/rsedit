DESCRIPTION
    The editor's commands are built out of two halves: a unit of text and
    something to do to it. Learn the units and the rest follows, because
    the same letter means the same thing wherever it appears.

        f  b            forward, backward
        a  e            start, end
        d  k            delete, kill
        Control         a character, or a line
        Meta            a word, or a paragraph
        Control-Meta    a balanced expression

    So C-f is a character forward, M-f a word forward, C-M-f an expression
    forward. C-d deletes a character, M-d kills a word, C-M-k kills an
    expression, and C-k kills to the end of the line.

    Killing is cutting, and what you kill can be put back: the `killing'
    page is about that, and about the region. The `indentation' page is
    about Tab and Return.

MOVING
        C-f  C-b        a character
        M-f  M-b        a word
        C-n  C-p        a line, keeping the column
        C-a  C-e        to the start or the end of the line
        M-}  M-{        a paragraph
        M-<  M->        to the start or the end of the buffer
        C-M-f  C-M-b    a balanced expression
        M-g g           to a line, by number

    A word is a run of letters, digits and underscores. A paragraph ends
    at a blank line. A balanced expression is a list, a string or an atom
    such as a name or a number, with any prefix in front of it, such as the
    quote in 'foo.

    What counts as a bracket, a string or a comment comes from the mode:
    `set-syntax-pairs' and `set-comment-syntax' in the `modes' page. A mode
    that says nothing still gets the three kinds of bracket, double quotes
    and backslash. A bracket inside a string or a comment is text, so C-M-f
    steps over (message "a )") in one move.

    C-M-f stops at the end of the list it is in. C-M-u goes up out of the
    list, to its opening bracket, and C-M-d down into the next one. M-x
    up-list goes up and out the other way, past the closing bracket.

COUNTS
    A number before a command means "do it that many times": C-u 20 C-f
    goes forward twenty characters, and C-u 3 M-d kills three words. C-u
    on its own means four, and C-u C-u sixteen. A count reaches the
    commands that take one: the motions by character, word, line,
    paragraph and expression, the kills by word and by expression, C-o,
    and C-x e. The others, and a character you type, happen once.

    For the motions a negative count goes the other way: C-u - 2 C-n goes
    up two lines, as (next-line -2) does. Zero goes nowhere.

TYPING
    A character you type inserts itself. A run of typing undoes a word or
    two at a time, not a character at a time.

    Backspace deletes the character behind point and C-d the one in
    front. Deleting does not save what it deletes; killing does.

PAIRS
    Typing an opening bracket inserts the closing one too, with point
    between them, and typing the closing bracket then steps over the one
    already there. Deleting either half of an empty pair, with Backspace
    or C-d, deletes both; a pair with only spaces or tabs inside counts as
    empty, and one with anything else inside loses only the half deleted.
    A double quote pairs the same way, and typing the quote that ends a
    string steps over the one already there.

    No partner is added in front of a word, since typing ( before foo is
    about to wrap it; nor when the list is already closed further on; nor
    inside a comment or a string. A closing bracket typed where the list
    is closed further on goes to that bracket rather than adding one.
    Pasted text is never paired.

    The pairs are the mode's, the same ones C-M-f follows. Pairing is on in
    fundamental-mode and rust-mode, and `electric-pair-enable' turns it on
    in another mode. In rust-mode quotes are not paired, since 'a is a
    lifetime there. Setting `electric-pair-mode' to nil turns all of it
    off, and `electric-pair-skip-self' only the stepping over.

LINES
        C-o             open a blank line above this one
        C-x C-o         delete the blank lines around here
        M-^             join this line to the one above
        C-x C-t         swap this line with the one above

    C-o and M-x open-line-below indent the new line as Tab would. C-x C-o
    leaves one blank line in a run of them, deletes a blank line on its
    own, and on a line with text deletes the blank lines after it. M-^
    leaves one space at the join, or none after an opening bracket or
    before a closing one. M-x duplicate-line puts a copy of the line below
    it.

CASE AND SPACE
        M-u  M-l        upcase, downcase the word in front
        M-c             capitalise it
        M-<space>       leave exactly one space here
        M-\             delete the spaces and tabs around here

    With a region, M-u, M-l and M-c change the whole region instead. Both
    space commands stay on one line, so they never join two.

TRANSPOSING
        C-t             swap the characters either side of point
        M-t             swap the words either side of point
        C-x C-t         swap this line with the one above

    C-t and M-t leave point after both, so pressing again carries the same
    character or word on, one more step forward. C-x C-t moves this line
    up and point with it, so pressing again moves it up again. At the end
    of a line C-t swaps the two characters before point, which fixes a typo
    you have just made.

ZAPPING
    M-z and a character kills everything up to and including the next one
    of that character: M-z ) kills to the end of a call. C-y puts it back.

COMMENTS
    M-; comments out the region, or the line when there is no region, and
    takes the comments off again when every line with text on it already
    has one. The comment opener goes at the indentation of the least
    indented line, so the block keeps its shape. C-x C-; does the line
    point is on, whatever is selected.

    How a comment is written comes from the mode, from
    `set-comment-syntax'. In a language with only block comments, M-; and
    C-x C-; wrap the lines in one. A mode that has said nothing gets a
    message rather than a guess.

    M-x comment-indent starts a comment at the end of the line, beside the
    code, or goes into the one already there. `comment-region' and
    `uncomment-region' go one way only, without deciding: they and
    comment-indent need a mode with line comments.

UNDOING
        C-/             undo, also C-_ and C-x u
        C-?             redo
        C-g             give up what is half done

    Undo takes back one command at a time, and moves point to where the
    change was. After an undo a plain u undoes again, and after a redo a
    plain r redoes again. A new edit after an undo forgets what could have
    been redone. Some terminals cannot send C-?: there, use M-x redo, or
    bind it to a key that arrives.

    The history keeps up to a mebibyte of deleted text in each buffer, and
    lets the oldest changes go past that; M-x set-undo-limit changes it.
    Code that makes several edits can split them into steps with
    `undo-boundary'.

    C-g abandons a key sequence typed halfway, a count, and the region. It
    does not stop a command that is already running.

REPEATING
    C-x z repeats the last command, and pressing z again repeats it again.
    For anything longer, record what you did and play it back: see the
    `macros' page.

LIMITS
    The Delete, Home, End, Page Up and Page Down keys and the function keys
    do nothing, and cannot be bound: the editor does not receive them.
    C-d, C-a, C-e, C-v and M-v do their work.

EXAMPLES
    Insert text from Lisp, several pieces at once:

        (insert "total: " 42 "\n")

    The line point is on, and its number:

        (list (line-number-at-point) (current-line))

    Do something at the end of the buffer and come back:

        (let ((here (point)))
          (goto-char (point-max))
          (insert "\n;; end\n")
          (goto-char here))

    Pair brackets in C as well, but not quotes:

        (electric-pair-enable 'cc-mode)
        (put 'cc-mode 'electric-pair-inhibit-quotes t)

    Keep every closing bracket you type, even next to one already there:

        (setq electric-pair-skip-self nil)

    Give the commands that have no key one of yours:

        (define-key nil "C-c d" 'duplicate-line)
        (define-key nil "C-c ;" 'comment-indent)

    Comment out a whole buffer from Lisp:

        (comment-region (point-min) (point-max))
