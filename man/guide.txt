NAME
    guide - start here

DESCRIPTION
    This is a text editor with a Lisp interpreter inside it. Almost
    everything above the bare mechanics is written in that Lisp, in files you
    can read and change, and the parts that are not are the parts that have
    to work before any of it loads.

    If you have used Emacs, the keys are its keys and you can skip to
    `rsedit'. If you have not, read `first-steps' and then come back.

THE GUIDE
    Read a page with C-h m and its name. These are meant in this order.

        first-steps       the screen, moving, typing, saving, undoing
        editing           the editing vocabulary, and the kill ring
        files             opening, saving, directories, going back
        searching         finding things, and replacing them
        getting-help      how to ask the editor about itself
        completion        Tab, and M-x
        keymaps           what a key is, and how to change one
        modes             what a buffer is being edited *as*
        configuration     making it yours
        writing-commands  when a setting is not enough

THE REFERENCE
    What a doc string cannot say: how the pieces fit, and why they are the
    shape they are.

        rsedit            the editor's own shape, and the help tree
        buffers           what is being edited
        windows           what shows a buffer
        keymaps           how a key becomes a command
        modes             what colouring and keys a buffer gets
        completion        how candidates are found and shown
        macros            record what you pressed, press it again
        rectangles        blocks of text, cut by column
        overlays          a face on one particular span
        virtual-text      text shown and not in the buffer
        themes            how the colours are chosen
        compile           running a build, and jumping to its complaints
        background        work that must not happen on the drawing thread

    Every command and every variable also documents itself. C-h f names a
    function, C-h v a variable, C-h k a key, C-h a searches all of them.
    Those are always right about the editor you are running, which a page
    cannot promise.

IF YOU ONLY REMEMBER ONE THING
    C-g gets you out. A prompt you did not mean to open, a search you have
    finished with, a half-typed key sequence, a selection you want rid of --
    C-g, and you are back.
