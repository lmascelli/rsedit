NAME
    overlays - a face on one particular span of text

SYNOPSIS
    (make-overlay START END FACE &optional PRIORITY CATEGORY)
    (delete-overlay HANDLE)
    (remove-overlays &optional CATEGORY)
    (overlays-at POSITION)
    (overlay-face HANDLE)

DESCRIPTION
    A mode colours text by its shape: a word in a list is a keyword
    wherever it is. An overlay colours one span, here, for a reason of its
    own, and stays on that text while the buffer is edited. The editor's
    are the match M-% is asking about, the marks M-g n leaves, the bold and
    underlining of a system manual page, and vim's visual selection.

        (make-overlay 10 20 'error)

    draws the characters from 10 up to 20 in the face error, and returns a
    handle: a number that names the overlay. START and END may come in
    either order, and are kept within the buffer; an empty span makes
    nothing, and returns nil. FACE is any face, as `themes' says.

DRAW ORDER
    The mode's colouring first, then the overlays, then the region, so an
    overlay shows on a keyword and the region on everything. Where two
    overlap, the one with the higher PRIORITY is drawn last, and shows; of
    two with the same, the one made later. A manual page's emphasis is 10,
    the marks of M-g n 20, M-% and vim 100.

AS THE TEXT CHANGES
    Typing before an overlay carries it along. Typing at either end leaves
    the new text outside it, so a highlighted match stays the text that
    matched; typing inside it makes it longer. Deleting part of it makes it
    shorter, and deleting all of it removes it for good: undoing the
    deletion brings the text back and not the overlay. Reverting the buffer
    removes every overlay in it.

CATEGORIES
    CATEGORY names whatever made the overlay, and is overlay when it is not
    given. It is what lets a maker take back its own in one call, however
    many it made, without keeping their handles and without touching anyone
    else's:

        (remove-overlays 'my-marks)

    With no category `remove-overlays' removes every overlay in the buffer,
    and it says how many went. `delete-overlay' removes one, by its handle;
    a handle is never used again, so an old one names nothing rather than
    something else. `overlays-at' gives the handles of those that cover a
    position, lowest priority first -- an overlay covers its START and not
    its END -- and `overlay-face' the face one is drawn in.

LIMITS
    An overlay has a face and a priority, and nothing else: it cannot hide
    text or give it keys of its own. Text that is not in the buffer at all
    is `virtual-text'.

    A buffer shown in a floating window is drawn without its overlays; only
    the region and virtual text are drawn there.

EXAMPLES
    Draw the next ten characters as an error, as one of your marks:

        (make-overlay (point) (+ (point) 10) 'error 5 'my-marks)

    The faces of whatever covers point:

        (mapcar 'overlay-face (overlays-at (point)))

    Take your marks away again:

        (remove-overlays 'my-marks)
