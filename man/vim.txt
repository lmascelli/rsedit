NAME
    vim - modal editing, as Vim does it

SYNOPSIS
    (vim-mode &optional ENABLED)
    (vim-mode-enable)
    (vim-mode-disable)
    (vim-mode-auto)
    (vim-mode-refresh)
    (global-vim-mode &optional ENABLED)
    (vim-normal-state)
    (vim-default-buffer-p)
    vim-mode-modes
    vim-mode-excluded-modes
    vim-mode-predicate
    vim-shift-width
    vim-search-wrap
    vim-registers
    vim-search-pattern
    vim-search-direction
    vim-search-regexp
    vim-last-find
    vim-last-change
    vim-recording-register
    vim-last-macro-register

DESCRIPTION
    M-x vim-mode gives the buffer you are in the keys of Vim: they move and
    act in Normal state, type in Insert state, overwrite in Replace state
    and select in Visual state. The buffer keeps its major mode, and with it
    its colouring, its indentation and its hooks; only what the keys do
    changes. The status line says the state -- NORMAL, INSERT, REPLACE,
    VISUAL, V-LINE or V-BLOCK -- where mode-line-format has %s, as it has
    unless you change it.

    The default init.lisp loads the module, and leaves it off. M-x vim-mode
    turns it on, and off again; vim-mode-enable turns it on and
    vim-mode-disable off, whatever it was, and turning it off gives the
    buffer back the keymap it had. From Lisp, (vim-mode t), or a positive
    number, turns it on, and 0 or a negative number off.

    Each buffer has a state of its own, and a command half typed in one
    stays there. What vim remembers between commands is shared by all: the
    registers, the last search, the edit . repeats, the macros. Escape and
    C-[, and C-g and C-c in Normal state, cancel a command half typed, or
    go back to Normal state.

TURNING IT ON BY ITSELF
    (global-vim-mode t) turns vim on in every buffer it suits, those there
    are and those still to come, and (global-vim-mode -1) turns that off,
    leaving on what was turned on by hand. vim-mode-modes names major modes
    vim is on in, whatever global-vim-mode says:

        (setq vim-mode-modes '(rust-mode risp-mode))
        (vim-mode-refresh)

    A buffer suits when all of these hold:

        global-vim-mode is on, or its major mode is in vim-mode-modes
        its major mode is not in vim-mode-excluded-modes
        vim-mode-predicate is nil, or answers non-nil in the buffer
        it is not a prompt

    vim-mode-excluded-modes starts with the editor's listings and buffers
    of its own: dired, help, the manual, the lists of buffers and themes, a
    backtrace, the output of a command or a build, a list of matches, a
    prompt and the completion strip. vim-mode-predicate starts as
    vim-default-buffer-p, which accepts a buffer visiting a file, *scratch*,
    and any buffer whose name does not begin with a *.

    The rules are applied when a buffer gets a major mode, and when C-x C-w
    gives it a file; vim-mode-refresh applies them again to every buffer,
    after they have changed. A buffer turned on or off by vim-mode,
    vim-mode-enable or vim-mode-disable stays so whatever the rules say,
    until vim-mode-auto gives it back to them. Applying the rules leaves an
    Insert or Visual session, and a command half typed, as they were.

MOVING IN NORMAL STATE
    h j k l             Left, down, up, right; the arrow keys too.
    <space> <backspace> Right and left, within the line.
    w b e               Next word, previous word, end of word.
    W B E               The same with WORDS, separated by white space.
    ge                  End of the previous word.
    0 ^ $               Beginning, first nonblank, last character of line.
    _                   First nonblank on this line.
    |                   Column COUNT, counting from one.
    gg G                First line, last line, or line COUNT.
    + - <ret>           First nonblank of the next or the previous line.
    { }                 Paragraph backward and forward.
    %                   The bracket matching the first one from point on
                        the line: (, ), [, ], { or }.
    f F t T CHARACTER   To the character on this line, or just before it.
    ; ,                 That find again, the same way or the other.
    C-d C-u             Down and up half a frame.
    C-f C-b             Down and up a frame, nearly.
    C-e C-y             Scroll a line at a time.

    A count goes before a motion or an operator, or both: 3w, 2dd, d3w,
    2d3w. Up and down keep the column through shorter lines; sideways stays
    on the line. In Normal state point is on a character, the last one at
    most, unless the line is empty.

EDITING
    i a I A             Insert before or after point, at the first
                        nonblank, at the end of the line.
    o O                 Open a line below or above, indented as this one,
                        and insert.
    R                   Overwrite until Escape; <backspace> gives back what
                        was overwritten.
    d c y MOTION        Delete, change or yank what the motion covers.
    dd cc yy            The same on whole lines, COUNT of them.
    x X s               Delete forward, backward; substitute forward.
    D C S Y             Delete or change to the end of the line; change or
                        yank whole lines.
    r CHARACTER         Replace COUNT characters, without inserting.
    p P                 Put after or before point, in the register's shape.
    J                   Join lines, dropping the indentation at the join.
    > < = MOTION        Shift right, left, or reindent; >>, << and == act
                        on lines.
    gU gu g~ MOTION     Upper case, lower case, swap; gUU, guu and g~~ act
                        on lines.
    ~                   Swap the case of COUNT characters, or of the
                        selection.
    u C-r               Undo and redo, COUNT times.
    .                   The last edit again, here; a COUNT replaces its own.

    In Insert state keys type as they do without vim, so a mode's pairing
    and indentation work, and so does any character. C-w deletes the word
    before point, C-u the line before point, and C-h a character. A paste
    is one edit, and nothing is paired in it. A whole Insert or Replace
    session, with the c that began it, is undone at once, and . repeats it,
    paste and pairing included. i, a, I or A with a COUNT types the text
    COUNT times when you leave Insert state.

TEXT OBJECTS
    i/a w W             Inner/around word or WORD.
    i/a ( ) b           Inner/around parentheses.
    i/a [ ]             Inner/around brackets.
    i/a { } B           Inner/around braces.
    i/a < >             Inner/around angle brackets.
    i/a " ' `           Inner/around a pair of quotes on this line.
    i/a p               Inner/around paragraph.

    An object follows an operator, or selects in Visual state: ciw, daW,
    di(, yi", viw. A bracket object takes the innermost pair around point.

VISUAL STATE
    v V C-v             Select characters, lines, a block.
    o                   Swap point and the other end.
    gv                  Select again what was selected last.
    <esc>               Back to Normal state.
    d c y p P           Delete, change, yank, or put over the selection.
    > < = gU gu g~ ~    Act on the selected characters or lines.
    u U                 Lower or upper case.

    Both ends are part of the selection, and a line selection is of whole
    lines. A block can be deleted, yanked, put and changed: a change on one
    line is made on every row of the block when you leave Insert state. A
    block put pads short lines, and adds lines that are missing.

REGISTERS AND MACROS
    "a COMMAND          Use register a; the registers are shared by buffers.
    "A COMMAND          Append to register a.
    "_ COMMAND          Discard: no register changes, nor the kill ring.
    "0p                 Put the last yank, which deletions leave alone.
    "1p ... "9p         Put the deletions of lines, newest first.
    "-p                 Put the last deletion within a line.
    qa ... q            Record the keys pressed into register a.
    qA ... q            Append them to register a.
    @a @@               Play register a, or the macro played last.

    The unnamed register is used unless another is named, and every
    register remembers the shape of its text: characters, lines or a
    block. What is deleted or yanked goes to the kill ring too, and so to
    the system clipboard when the clipboard module sends it there. A macro
    is the keys as pressed, Insert state, Escape and the answers to prompts
    included, and a COUNT plays it COUNT times.

SEARCHES AND MARKS
    /PATTERN <ret>      Search forward.
    ?PATTERN <ret>      Search backward.
    n N                 Again, the same way or the other.
    * #                 Search for the word at point, forward or backward.
    m a                 Set mark a here.
    ` a, ' a            Go to mark a, or to the first nonblank of its line.

    A lowercase mark is this buffer's; an uppercase one remembers its
    buffer, and going to it goes there. A search goes round the end of the
    buffer unless vim-search-wrap is nil, and / or ? with nothing typed
    searches for the last pattern again. A search and a mark can follow an
    operator. A pattern is one of the editor's regular expressions, which
    the `regexps' page describes, not one of Vim's.

EX COMMANDS
    :w, :write, :w!     Save.
    :w PATH             Write to PATH, and visit it.
    :q, :quit           Quit, asking about unsaved work.
    :wq, :x, ZZ         Save, then quit if the save went through.
    :q!, ZQ             Quit without saving.
    :e, :e PATH         Open a file, asked for or given.
    :b NAME, :bd        Switch to buffer NAME; kill a buffer, asked for.
    :split, :vsplit     Split the window, below or right.
    :only, :close       Keep only this window; close it.
    :NUMBER             Go to that line.
    :set number         Line numbers.
    :set relativenumber Line numbers counted from point.
    :set nonumber       No line numbers.
    :set ignorecase     Searches ignore case.
    :set noignorecase   Searches mind case.
    :set wrapscan       Searches go round the end.
    :set nowrapscan     Searches stop at the end.
    :noh                Deactivate the mark.
    :help, :h           This page.

    :s/PATTERN/REPLACEMENT/FLAGS
        Replace the first match on this line, or with g every one; i and I
        ignore or mind case for this replacement. In REPLACEMENT & is the
        whole match, \1 to \9 its groups, \& an ampersand, \t a tab, and \n
        and \r a newline. The delimiter can be any character, and one
        escaped with \ is part of the text.

    :%s/.../.../g       On every line.
    :2,5s/.../.../g     On lines 2 to 5.
    :'<,'>s/.../.../g   On the lines of the last Visual selection.

    A substitution is undone at once. A pattern that is wrong, or a flag
    that is not one of these, is reported. In Visual state : leaves the
    selection, and '<,'> has to be typed.

WINDOWS AND THE REST
    C-w s, C-w v        Split below or right.
    C-w w               The next window.
    C-w o, C-w c        Keep only this window; close it.
    z KEY               Recenter.
    K                   The manual page named by the word at point.

SETTINGS
        vim-shift-width          the columns > and < shift by: 4
        vim-search-wrap          whether searches go round the end: t
        vim-mode-modes           the major modes vim is on in by itself
        vim-mode-excluded-modes  those it is never on in by itself
        vim-mode-predicate       what says whether a buffer suits

WHAT VIM REMEMBERS
    Shared by every buffer, and there to read from Lisp:

        vim-registers            the registers: ((NAME TEXT SHAPE) ...)
        vim-search-pattern       the last pattern searched for
        vim-search-direction     1 after /, -1 after ?
        vim-search-regexp        t: a pattern is a regular expression
        vim-last-find            the last f, F, t or T: (KEY CHARACTER)
        vim-last-change          the edit . repeats, in a form of vim's own
        vim-recording-register   the register keys are being recorded into
        vim-last-macro-register  the register @@ plays

KEYMAPS
    Normal and Visual state use the keymap of vim-normal-map, Insert state
    that of vim-insert-map and Replace state that of vim-replace-map, each
    put on the buffer as its local keymap. A key bound there with
    define-key replaces what vim does with it:

        (define-key 'vim-normal-map "Q" 'vim-mode-disable)

    In Normal state a printable key that does nothing is swallowed, not
    typed. A key that no map of vim binds does what it does without vim, so
    M-x works, and so does C-x C-s; and a question the editor asks takes
    its keys before vim does.

LIMITS
    This is much of Vim and not all of it. There is no Vimscript, no
    :global and no Ex range but those above, no folds, no jump list, no
    screen-relative motion, and nothing that reads the system clipboard:
    paste with the terminal. C-o in Insert state does what it does without
    vim, and opens a line.

    A mark is a position: text inserted before it moves away from it.

    In a block selection I and A insert on the line of point only, and a
    block change is made on every row only when its text is on one line. A
    count before R is ignored, and . does not repeat a substitution.

    = reindents with the indent module, which the default init.lisp loads.
    C-w h, j, k and l go to the next window, whichever way it lies, and z
    followed by any key recenters. The paging keys count by the frame's
    height, not the window's.

    ge from the first character of a word goes to the end of the word
    before the previous one, and dge deletes that much more.

    A count before @ or @@ counts for the macro's first command as well:
    3@a, where a deletes a character, deletes five.

    ap is ip: the blank lines after the paragraph are left out.

    C-u - before M-x vim-mode or M-x global-vim-mode turns it on rather
    than off; C-u -1 turns it off.

EXAMPLES
    Vim in every buffer it suits, from init.lisp:

        (global-vim-mode t)

    Only in Rust and Lisp buffers:

        (setq vim-mode-modes '(rust-mode risp-mode))
        (vim-mode-refresh)

    Only in buffers visiting files:

        (setq vim-mode-predicate (lambda () (buffer-file-name)))
        (vim-mode-refresh)

    > and < by two columns:

        (setq vim-shift-width 2)

    Q, which vim leaves unused, to turn vim off in the buffer:

        (define-key 'vim-normal-map "Q" 'vim-mode-disable)

    What register a holds:

        (assoc "a" vim-registers)
